Key Findings

The Biggest NBA Arena Cost Takeaways

Six quick takeaways from Action Network's 2026–27 ranking of NBA arena costs for families.

$551.86
Key Finding 01

Smoothie King Center Is the Cheapest NBA Arena for Families

Home of the New Orleans Pelicans, Smoothie King Center has the lowest family-of-four total at $551.86, which is $402.78 below the NBA average.

$1,930.55
Key Finding 02

Madison Square Garden Is the Most Expensive

A family-of-four package for the New York Knicks costs $1,930.55, which is $975.91 above the NBA average and $1,378.69 more than the Pelicans.

$954.64
Key Finding 03

The Average NBA Arena Visit Costs $954.64 for a Family of Four

Across all 30 teams, four tickets average $823.92, while food, drinks and parking add $130.72 to the family total.

23.20%
Key Finding 04

Knicks Fans Face the Highest Financial Burden

A family-of-four Knicks outing costs the equivalent of 23.20% of the New York metro’s median monthly household income, the highest share in the ranking. The Wizards have the lowest share at 6.97%.

+105.74%−23.54%
Key Finding 05

76ers Ticket Prices More Than Double as Portland Sees the Biggest Drop

The 76ers’ four-ticket cost rose 105.74%, from $723.61 to $1,488.76, the largest percentage increase from 2025–26 to 2026–27. The Trail Blazers had the biggest decline, falling 23.54% from $651.15 to $497.84.

$86.10
Key Finding 06

Chase Center Has the Highest Food-and-Drink Cost

Two beers, two soft drinks and four hot dogs at the Golden State Warriors’ Chase Center total $86.10. The NBA average for this concession bundle is $66.65.

How the Ranking Works

Methodology and Sources

Total Cost for a Family of Four

The ranking estimates what a family of four would pay for a regular-season NBA game in 2026–27. Each total includes four tickets, two beers, two soft drinks, four hot dogs and one parking space. Child admission is shown separately as supporting data.

For each team, the research sampled the four lowest available ticket prices for each month of the season. These four-ticket totals were averaged across the sampled months. The Knicks’ reported figure is the mean of the averages from Vivid Seats and SeatGeek.

Food and Drink

The supplied methodology uses prior-season concession prices from Action Network’s 2025 NBA family-cost research and applies a 2.5% annual inflation adjustment to estimate current-season costs. The package includes two beers, two soft drinks and four hot dogs.

Concession SourceAction Network 2025 ResearchThe previous-year study provides the concession-price baseline used in this analysis.
Stated Adjustment+2.5%Previous price × 1.025. The supplied dataset’s final prices are used as provided.

Parking

The methodology identifies the lowest-priced parking ticket for the nearest garage to each arena using Vivid Seats. The supplied parking figures are added once per family package.

Child Admission and Supporting Data

Child admission policies were collected from official arena websites and are shown alongside the costs. They do not reduce the four-ticket package. For New Orleans, the supplied policy leaves children’s entry to the event client’s discretion, so no universal free-entry age is assumed. Hat prices were collected from official team stores but are not included in the total.

Year-Over-Year Ticket Price Changes

The ticket price comparison pairs each team’s supplied 2025–26 four-ticket cost with its final 2026–27 four-ticket cost. Dollar change is the current-season cost minus the previous-season cost; percentage change divides that difference by the previous-season cost. The surge view includes positive changes, highest percentage first. The drop view includes negative changes, largest percentage decline first. These are comparisons of the supplied season estimates, not a matched-seat or matched-game price index. Family-package totals are not used in this comparison. Across all 30 teams, the unweighted average four-ticket price rose from $730.44 to $823.92, an increase of 12.80%. This is the percentage change in the league-wide average, not the average of the individual teams’ percentage changes.

Local Household Affordability

The financial burden ranking divides each team’s 2026–27 family-of-four cost by one-twelfth of its local metro’s annual median household income, then multiplies by 100. Higher shares indicate a greater cost relative to local household income. U.S. teams use 2024 American Community Survey 1-Year metro median household income. Toronto uses an approximate comparable Statistics Canada benchmark converted to 2024 USD, so its cross-border comparison is indicative. Monthly income is an annual benchmark divided by 12, not a measure of disposable income or the actual earnings of a team’s fans. Rankings follow the supplied affordability table; displayed percentages are rounded to two decimal places.

Ranking Notes

All 30 teams are ranked by total family cost, from lowest to highest, with the 15 cheapest and 15 most expensive shown separately. The NBA average is the unweighted mean of all 30 totals. Ticket sample sizes vary by team and are shown in each team’s expandable cost breakdown; they are not used to weight the league average. Arena names, policies and prices are reproduced from the supplied 2026–27 dataset. Prices are estimates and can vary by game and availability; admission policies should be checked with the arena before booking.

Total = four tickets + two beers + two soft drinks + four hot dogs + one parking space. Accommodation, travel and merchandise are excluded. Dollar amounts follow the supplied dataset; a separate currency-conversion basis was not provided.
